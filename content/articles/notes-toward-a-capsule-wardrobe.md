DRAFT-SENTINEL unfinished capsule notes. A capsule wardrobe is a small set of clothes that all work together. I like the idea a lot more than I like the discipline it requires.

These are working notes. Half-formed, probably wrong in places, and definitely too fond of burgundy.

<!--more-->

## The palette comes first

If everything has to go with everything, the colours have to agree before the garments do. I'm working from what the burgundy experiments taught me: one dark anchor, a couple of quiet neutrals, and one accent.

- **Anchor:** burgundy, obviously.
- **Neutrals:** navy and an off-white cream.
- **Bridge:** camel, to warm things up.
- **Accent:** olive, used sparingly.

Five colours feels like a lot for a capsule. I may cut one. Probably not the burgundy.

## The pieces, so far

The rough list, which changes every time I open this file:

1. A navy unstructured jacket, half-canvassed, soft shoulder.
2. Two pairs of trousers: one wool, one heavier cotton.
3. A cream oxford shirt and a burgundy knit.
4. A camel overcoat for the cold months.
5. Something olive. I haven't decided what. A chore coat, maybe.

The test for each item is simple: can I name three other things in the capsule it pairs with? If not, it doesn't get in.

## Open questions

- How many tops is too few before laundry becomes a crisis?
- Do I count shoes? I suspect I have to.
- Is a capsule still a capsule if I keep sewing new things for it?

> A capsule is a constraint, and constraints are where the good ideas hide.

## Still to figure out

Fabric weights across seasons. Whether the knit should be lambswool or something smoother. How much texture variation the palette can take before it stops feeling like one wardrobe.

There's also the question of making versus buying. Sewing the whole capsule myself is tempting, but it's also the fastest way to never finish it. I suspect the answer is to make the pieces where fit matters most, the jacket and the trousers, and be relaxed about the rest.

And I need a rule for retiring things. A capsule only stays small if something leaves when something arrives. One in, one out, ideally to someone who'll wear it.

More soon, once I stop rearranging the list.
