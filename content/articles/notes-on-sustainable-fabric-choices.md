I don't have a tidy answer on sustainable fabric. Anyone who claims to probably sells something. What I have is a set of habits that make me feel less guilty at the cutting table.

These are notes, not rules. I'm still learning which ones hold up.

<!--more-->

## Start with longevity

The greenest garment is the one you already own, and the second greenest is the one you'll wear for a very long time. So my first question about any cloth isn't where it came from. It's whether it'll survive being loved.

That means looking at weave density, fibre quality, and how it ages. A tightly woven wool will outlast a loose one. A good linen softens over years instead of falling apart.

## Why I keep reaching for linen

[Linen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linen) comes from the flax plant, which is generally regarded as a low-fuss crop. It's strong, breathable, and gets better with washing.

The craft details I like:

- It presses beautifully, holding a crease for seams and hems.
- It frays readily, so I finish seams carefully, often with French seams.
- It wrinkles. I've made peace with this. The wrinkles are part of the look.

The downside is that it doesn't drape like silk or stretch like a knit. It wants simple, generous shapes.

## Sourcing without paralysis

It's easy to freeze up trying to find the perfect source. I've started working from a short list of questions instead:

1. Is it a natural fibre, or a blend I'll never be able to recycle?
2. Can I find out anything about how it was made?
3. Is it deadstock or leftover from someone else's production?
4. Will I actually use all of it?

That last one is underrated. Buying exactly the yardage I need, and planning a lay to minimise waste, does more good than agonising over a label.

## Use the scraps

Offcuts go into a bin, and the bin becomes facings, pocket bags, bias binding, and patches. Small pieces of good cloth are too nice to throw away.

> Waste is just fabric you haven't found a use for yet.

None of this makes me a saint. I still buy cloth on impulse. But I buy less of it, I use more of it, and I make things I intend to keep. That feels like the right direction.
